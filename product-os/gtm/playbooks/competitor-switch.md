# Playbook: Competitor Switch

**Trigger:** one of — a fixed-7 competitor in the tech stack (technographic); a
competitor named in a reply or call; a negative review on a competitor's page;
inbound from a competitor's market.

## Step 1 — Identify the scenario

| Scenario | Situation |
|---|---|
| **A** | Uses a competitor, hasn't heard of Ockham — technographic fit, not looking |
| **B** | Active evaluation — comparing Ockham to a competitor |
| **C** | Left a negative review of a competitor — high intent, specific pain |
| **D** | Under contract with a competitor, not evaluating |

## Scenario A — uses a competitor, cold

Pull the battlecard ([`../battlecards/`](../battlecards/)). Lead with the
**insight** about what teams at their stage find limiting — **don't name the
competitor** in the first touch. For an ops competitor, the through-line is
"separate security product / budget / team." For a security competitor, it's "no
observability side / assumes a SOC or CISO you don't have."

> Don't say "I saw you use [X] and we're better." Lead with the pattern, not the
> comparison.

## Scenario B — active evaluation

Act within 24 h.
1. Understand which dimension of the evaluation matters most to them.
2. Pull the battlecard; identify the 2–3 differentiators relevant to *that*
   dimension.
3. Build a targeted side-by-side on their stated criteria — not a generic feature
   matrix.
4. **Equip the champion**: what to say internally about [X], why Ockham fits
   where they're going, a reference story that mirrors their situation.

## Scenario C — negative review (high intent)

Act within 24 h. Write a short first touch that references the pain they
described (without revealing you read the review), connects it to what Ockham
solves, and stays brief — this is a warm signal, not a cold pitch.

## Scenario D — under contract

Don't push for a meeting. Send something genuinely useful (an industry insight, a
framework) with no ask beyond continuing the conversation. Set a task to follow
up ~60 days before likely renewal — then run the renewal-price-shock playbook.

## Keep battlecards current

After every competitive **win** or **loss**, update the battlecard within 24 h:
the deciding factor, what the competitor said, what tipped it. After 5+ against
the same vendor, summarise the pattern. A battlecard from a planning session is
worth a fraction of one written after a real deal.
