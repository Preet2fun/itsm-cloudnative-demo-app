# Lens 4 — Cohort Compare

**Purpose:** show whether a need is universal or segment-specific — the question
behind most scoping decisions.

**Question:** Across these segments, what's shared, what differs, what's unique?

**Inputs:** 2+ segments named from a column in the data (tier, geography,
vertical, plan); the topic, or "all themes"; the data source.

**Do:**
1. Pull each segment's top themes independently.
2. Lay them out in three views:

   | View | Meaning | Product move |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Shared** | appears across all compared segments | build once |
   | **Differential** | present for some, absent / minor for others | the product-strategy question lives here |
   | **Unique** | only one segment | scope to it, or defer |

3. For differential themes, note the likely driver. Label it **HYPOTHESIS**.

**Produce:** the three-view table · per-segment top themes · 2–3 quotes per
distinctive theme · Limitations (segment sample sizes).

**Feeds:** `ai-prd/` stage 02 (Users & JTBD) and template §4 (Users, Personas &
Use Cases) — who differs and how; `ai-discovery/` stage 03 (affected personas).
A strongly differential theme is a signal to scope the PRD to a segment, not
ship broad.
