# Battlecard: Dynatrace

**One-line:** Enterprise observability with Davis AI and an SRE agent; runs the
**"deterministic AI" / "you can check its work"** play — the same explainability
message Ockham uses.
**Market position:** Leader (enterprise). **Typical buyer:** large enterprise
platform teams; multi-year contracts.

## Their strengths (be honest)

- Davis AI is mature; the explainability / "check its work" message is strong and
  **market-validated** — this is a reason to use the play, not avoid it
  ([`../../context-hub/positioning.md`](../../context-hub/positioning.md) § proof
  chains).
- Deep automatic instrumentation; config-level remediation and prevention.

## Their weaknesses (for our ICP)

- **Enterprise-priced and enterprise-shaped** — heavy to deploy and operate for a
  20–80-person IT org.
- **Security is still separate** (Dynatrace has application security, but the
  converged ops+security-for-a-no-SOC-team story isn't theirs).
- Sold to large platform orgs with dedicated owners; our ICP is the opposite.

## Why an ICP account switches

- Same explainability guarantee, without the enterprise weight and price.
- One team, one budget, uptime + security — not a platform that assumes a
  dedicated observability function.

## Objection handling

> **"Dynatrace already does deterministic AI."**
> They do, and it's a good message — we're not arguing against it, we're arguing
> it should be affordable and operable for a team without a platform org. Same
> "check its work" guarantee, converged with security, priced for a 40-person IT
> team.

## Detect the account uses them

- `dynatrace` / `ruxit` in tech-stack fingerprint
- OneAgent references in job posts
- Enterprise-contract language; "Dynatrace administrator" roles
